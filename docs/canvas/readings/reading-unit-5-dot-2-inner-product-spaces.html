Inner Product Spaces

Introduction to Inner Product Spaces

Inner product spaces generalize the concept of dot product from Rn to more abstract vector spaces. They provide a way to measure angles, lengths, and orthogonality in these spaces, allowing us to extend geometric intuition beyond standard Euclidean space.

In data science, inner product spaces form the mathematical foundation for many algorithms and techniques, including kernel methods, function approximation, and signal processing. Understanding these spaces helps us develop more sophisticated models and gain deeper insights into the structure of data.

In linear regression, the prediction ŷ = Xβ is the orthogonal projection of the target vector y onto the column space of the feature matrix X. This minimizes the sum of squared errors ||y - ŷ||2, which is why it's called the least squares method.

Applications in Data Science

Linear Regression and Least Squares

Inner product spaces provide the mathematical foundation for linear regression:

  • The linear regression problem minimizes the squared error ||y - Xβ||2, which is the squared norm in the standard inner product space
  • The normal equations XTXβ = XTy express that the residual y - Xβ is orthogonal to the column space of X
  • The solution β = (XTX)-1XTy computes the orthogonal projection of y onto the column space of X

Principal Component Analysis (PCA)

PCA relies heavily on inner product concepts:

  • The covariance matrix C = XTX/(n-1) contains inner products between features
  • Principal components are orthogonal eigenvectors of the covariance matrix
  • The projection onto the principal component subspace minimizes the reconstruction error in the Euclidean norm
  • The variance explained by each principal component is related to the eigenvalues of the covariance matrix
